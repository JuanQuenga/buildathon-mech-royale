# Mech Royale public replay research

## What the public data covers

The organizers expose full match telemetry for the public cast and browser lobbies, including real team submissions in the cast's Top 16 Showdown. The Showdown is a 16-bot free-for-all exhibition. The discovered public feeds yielded zero matches in the scored practice format of one entrant against the cooperating 15-bot house squad. The public practice board gives one best score per team. It cannot identify how an entrant earned that score. [Track, practice and final rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders) · [Cast implementation](https://watch.joinbuilders.org/show/show.js) · [Practice standings feed](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/standings/builders)

The standings fetched just after 11:43 p.m. ET on November 20 showed **5.58 at rank 1, 4.29 at rank 32 and 4.06 at rank 50**, with 10 scores above 5.00 and a 1.15 starter baseline. The standings had 143 rows. The supplied 5.48 and 3.81 values describe an earlier snapshot. The team's ~3.6 is a local simulator result. A true score above 5 requires at least **241 placement points over 48 matches**. For example, 47 elevenths and one tenth do it, as do 16 wins, one fifteenth and 31 sixteenths. The mean alone cannot distinguish consistency from a high-variance strategy. ## Capture and measurement

I retrieved public HTML, JavaScript and JSON with plain GETs and inspected them as text or parsed data. For telemetry I used Node's built-in WebSocket client with at most one active connection per route. I sent no actions or application messages. No downloaded code ran, no account was used, and no submissions were made.

For each socket match, I required a header, frame 0, all scheduled snapshot ticks and the final tick, plus a `result` row. I deduplicated by replay seed and used the cast's airing label alongside the roster to identify the format. Frames arrive at 10 Hz, with event timestamps on 20 Hz ticks. I used the packed field names from each header. Gun hit rates below divide damage events by gun shots and identify target type where possible. Hit distance is 3-D separation around impact, using the adjacent snapshots, so it is an approximation and measures successful hits only. A roof sample requires grounded feet at a mapped prism top within 0.12 m and at least 3 m above terrain, with the mech center inside that roof's polygon. This conservative definition can miss roof-edge overhangs. Map edge means within 30 m of a map boundary. Safe zone edge means the inner 30 m of the current circle. [Arena map](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/arena/v2/world.json)



The capture spans 11:43 p.m. ET on November 20 to 12:04 a.m. on November 21. There are **eight unique complete socket matches** with **15,019 frames**, plus the **2,098-frame** static demo. The two feeds both carried lobby seed 236856699, which I counted once. The partial tails at connection cutoffs were excluded. All raw captures and inspected responses are in [replays](mech-royale/replays).

## What we can verify about the house squad

The official practice rules confirm a 4 easy / 4 medium / 4 hard / 3 expert squad, shared vision, friendly non-engagement, one early attacker, escalation as the squad loses members and universal engagement late. They provide no timing threshold, loss-to-attacker formula, targeting range or per-tier accuracy. I found no scored practice trace to measure when the first house attacker detects, fires at or damages the entrant, or when other tiers join. The public match frames show positions and hits across the arena. They lack per-bot observations, target choice, engagement state and squad sight sharing. [Official practice rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders)

### Current-balance check fixture

The fixture has 302 records, comprising one `hello`, 300 observations and one `end`. It is slot 3's view from 0 to 36.8 simulated seconds with several gaps. The simulator's calibration identifies the observer as a house bot. The fixture does not label the match mode or either combatant's tier. Slot 3 first saw mech 10 at **4.9 s**, hit it with the gun at **5.7 s**, and took **315 laser damage at 7.9 s**. All ten outgoing hits and all 13 damage events in the fixture involved that pair. Slot 3 finished 13th with zero kills. The kit config gives 252 m/s bullets, 45 HP/s repair after 5 s without taking or dealing damage, and 5 s of tracking after the last sighting. That tracking rule keeps the target's current position in the observation during its memory window. The fixture does not establish when the practice squad loses track or whether one bot can refresh another's memory. ### House bots in current browser lobbies

Six complete, unique lobbies contained 89 house-bot seats and seven human seats. The house lineup was `house:expert` plus four other `house:probe-*` archetypes. Other mechs fought each other. This makes the lobbies useful for checking current mechanics and measuring environmental damage around real movement, with different opponents and an accelerated zone clock.

- **Opening and weapons.** House bots first hit another mech at a median of **12.55 s** (10th to 90th percentile **3.70–50.55 s**). Some hits were against humans and others were between house bots. Of 89 house seats, **71** took a secondary within 30 s and **77** ever took one. Among takers the median was **4.15 s**. First pickups split into 25 lasers, 28 lightning and 24 mortars.
- **Range and accuracy.** House gun fire yielded **1,825 mech hits from 6,899 shots (26%)**. Counting drones and crates raises it to **2,314/6,899 (34%)**. The 18 expert-labeled seats landed **301/601** gun shots on mechs (50%) and **344/601** on any target (57%). Median house gun hit distance was **65 m** (10th to 90th percentile **32–112 m**). Median hit distances were **80 m** for laser and **76 m** for mortar. Shots and routes were observable in this mode. Practice tier assignment and accuracy remain unknown.
- **Jets and roofs.** The median house bot spent **30%** of its life airborne and **5.9%** both airborne and flagged as boosting. **22/89** had a sampled roof position. House bots landed **39** hits while standing on roofs and **20** hits on standing roof occupants. A roof can change the fight geometry. These counts do not establish whether house bots chose their jump to pursue a target.
- **Environmental losses.** Of 84 eliminated house bots, **two died to the zone** at **95.05** and **107.55 s**, and **three died to drones** at **39.00**, **57.65**, and **81.50 s**. The two zone deaths and the first two drone deaths followed zero recorded hits from human slots. The 81.50 s victim took 18 human hits. House-on-house damage was possible in all six matches, including for those four victims. One human also died to the zone at 166.35 s. ## Simulator changes supported by the evidence

- **Read the zone schedule from the observation.** The Showdown header agrees with the kit schedule. The browser lobbies used half-length hold and shrink intervals with the same sequence of radii. The first shrink ended at 55 s in the lobbies and 110 s in the Showdown. The last shrink was scheduled to end at 207.5 s and 415 s, respectively. A fixed lobby clock would distort practice routing. Both newer feeds had median gun bullet speeds of **252 m/s**, matching the kit config. The old demo's 140 m/s is unsuitable for current lead prediction. [Rulebook](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md) · [Cast telemetry](mech-royale/replays/cast-live.jsonl) · [Lobby telemetry](mech-royale/replays/play-room3-live.jsonl)
- **Keep the practice firing cadence open.** The check fixture's gun cadence is consistent with six-tick gaps while firing continuously. All 32 team-bot seats in the two Showdowns fired guns on both tick parities. Before an observed gun-rate upgrade, consecutive gun shots were often five ticks apart. Across gaps of at most ten ticks, I counted **1,046 five-tick gaps**, **141 four-tick gaps** and **24 six-tick gaps**. Browser lobby house bots also produced five-tick gaps. This directly contradicts the simulator assumption if it is applied to every mode. Practice could still use the check fixture cadence. Use the existing `--sim fire_first_tick_only=false` switch for a faster-firing sensitivity case before interpreting weapon or survivability changes. [Simulator timing assumptions](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/ASSUMPTIONS.md) · [Calibrated check cadence](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/CALIBRATION.md) · [Cast telemetry](mech-royale/replays/cast-live.jsonl)
- **Lightning chaining is observable in the current lobby feed.** The older demo had 47 zaps and every one hit exactly one target. In the six lobbies I counted **16 two-target arcs in 705 zaps**, with no three-target arc. The chain can occur under the current balance. These recordings do not resolve the cone threshold, the number of available chain jumps in each scenario or the squad's target-selection policy. [Calibration](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/CALIBRATION.md) · [Lobby telemetry](mech-royale/replays/play-room3-live.jsonl)
- **Treat squad settings as experimental variables.** The simulator currently picks one early engager, adds one per dead teammate, enables everyone at 300 s, gives passive bots zero retaliation and zero point-blank defense, and caches shared positions for 8 s. The published rules support the broad escalation pattern. Each numerical setting and the side effects of attacking a passive house bot remain unverified. The rulebook's 5 s actor tracking timer is an observation rule and gives no value for the squad's shared tracking timeout. The local starter result (0.90 on 48 seeds) and official baseline (1.15) are two aggregates. Earlier simulator calibration saw a change from 1.00 to 0.22 when it enabled four seconds of retaliation, which shows why a policy can overfit this setting. [Simulator squad settings](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/README.md) · [Assumptions](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/ASSUMPTIONS.md) · [Calibration](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/CALIBRATION.md) · [Practice rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders)

## Ranked practice experiments

The public data supports these as paired simulator experiments. A local increase is evidence about the tested scenarios. Official practice remains the missing validation.

1. **Avoid losing the first duel and finish it when the isolation holds.** Test the existing `multi_retreat` switch with a reachable cover requirement and a low-HP finish exception. Keep aim toward the confirmed attacker during retreat. Compare an opener that takes isolated duels with one that reserves fire for defense until equipped. The official rules give a one-attacker opening, and the current fixture shows a mech hit within six seconds. The eight top-four exhibition seats took fewer opening hits at the median and secured most kills. The local diagnosis already finds low-placement losses to one early attacker while the bot spreads damage across passive bots. Track rear damage, distinct incoming attackers by 30 s, eliminations before death and placement. The earlier 32-seed `multi_retreat=0.35` test was inconclusive, so test the combined policy on a larger paired sample. [Existing policy](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/bot/brain.py) · [Local match diagnosis and experiments](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md)
2. **Require positive evidence before committing to FFA behavior.** The current classifier treats one unexplained death before 200 s as enough to enter FFA mode. The official rules rule out deliberate house-on-house combat in practice. They allow zone and drone kills. The public lobby replays include early environmental deaths without a player hit. Test keeping the classification provisional after a single unexplained death, and promote on repeated damage exchanges between other mechs when those can be attributed. Measure both false FFA switches in squad scenarios and time to detect real FFA. [Mode inference](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/bot/brain.py) · [Practice rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders)
3. **Choose the first secondary by reachable exposure, then test a laser preference.** Keep the existing weapon search. Redirect it toward a spawn-specific pickup along the escape route and leave if the detour exposes the bot to another attacker. Six of eight top finishers in the exhibitions ended with a laser. Nine of 16 demo bots found a second weapon within 30 s. The local weapon-first change increased two-weapon finishes from 84% to 91% with a neutral placement result, so test route and weapon choice as well as acquisition frequency. Compare a laser preference with the existing pick order by time to first pickup, damage taken en route and whether it improves the first duel. Use 252 m/s gun lead and count damage to mechs separately from drones and crates. [Weapon policy](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/bot/brain.py) · [Local weapon-first result](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md)
4. **Keep the wall-aware rotation and make healing and roof commitments prove their exit.** Use the existing next-circle ETA to give each fight, roof hop or pickup a latest safe departure time. When resetting a fight, stop taking and dealing damage for 5 s, then allow 45 HP/s for repair inside the zone. Recovering from 700 HP to the 1,400 base maximum takes about 21 s including the wait. All eight top-four seats stayed inside the circle in their last 120 s, and seven used no sampled roof position. The demo and lobbies show that house bots can jet, stand on roofs and land hits on roof occupants. Test roofs as conditional exits or firing positions under both limited and persistent enemy tracking. Keep the ground route available. [Navigation and jet changes](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/docs/INTEGRATION_NOTES.md) · [Repair rules](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md)
5. **Select for placement across several plausible directors.** Use paired seeds and an untouched holdout, and test the number of initial attackers, extra attackers per loss, all-engage time, retaliation, shared tracking and the observed alternative gun cadence. Use large batches for small effects because the local results show a 0.41-point spread across repeats on the same 32 seeds. Report the placement histogram, points, time and cause of deaths, and pre-death house environmental casualties. For a local baseline of roughly 173 total points, exceeding 5 on 48 seeds takes about 68 additional points. Prefer gains that survive the director changes and rescue early exits. [Local evaluation repeatability](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/eval/RESULTS.md) · [Squad configuration](repos/buildathon-mech-royale/sim/README.md)

## What remains unknown

No public data in this investigation establishes the scored practice squad's first engagement time, weapon pickups by tier, firing-distance or accuracy distributions, exact escalation timing, zone/drone attrition without entrant damage, pursuit onto roofs, or track loss after line of sight breaks. The official board gives best-run means only, so entrant opening and closing behavior and placement histograms for a 5+ practice score remain unknown. The signed-in submission pages and the Sunday final broadcast were outside the observed sample.
