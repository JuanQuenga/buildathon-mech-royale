# Mech Royale build handoff

Verified November 20, 2026, after kickoff. Submission cutoff is **Sunday, November 22, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET**. The track pays $5,000 to the winner by final score, with organizer verification. Build a bot that pilots one mech in a 16-mech, 3D battle royale on the Ohio State campus. Your bot reads observations and sends intentions. The server simulates the match. [Mech Royale track](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders) · [Buildathon rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon#rules)

The build priority is navigation that keeps the bot alive and in the next safe circle, followed by accurate, selective fire and a policy for when to heal or take loot. **Practice and the final require different tactics.** Practice is one entrant against a cooperative house squad. Qualifying and the series are free-for-alls. Use official practice for qualification and choose a policy that can also beat other entrants head-to-head.

## Scoring and tournament

| Round | Field and seeds | Score and advancement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practice | 48 matches per submission. One entrant plus 4 easy, 4 medium, 4 hard, and 3 expert house bots. Seeds are private and fixed through the event. Entrant slot and spawn vary by match. | “mean of (16 - place) over every match in a run” per the track. The Python example is the 1.15 baseline. The practice board displays each team's best. The selected entry's own score determines its qualifying position. Scorer version `builders-3`. |
| Qualifying | Top 32 locked practice entries. Each team plays 16 16-mech free-for-alls against other qualifiers. Lineups and seeds are made after cutoff. | Top 16 by qualifying mean of `16 − place` advance. The organizers announce the finalists without their scores. |
| Final series | All 16 finalists play together on new seeds for 64 matches. The last four are shown live and have the same weight. | The mean of `16 − place` in the series determines finalist rank and the prize. Practice and qualifying scores have no weight here. |

Placement is reverse death order. First place earns 15, last earns 0. Same-tick deaths share a place. If the match clock expires, survivors rank by HP. Kills help indirectly through loot and elimination. All listed score ties go to earlier submissions. Eliminated qualifiers rank below finalists by qualifying score, followed by the rest in practice-board order. Published final standings encode non-finalists with negative scores and remain hidden until the last match. [Track scoring and final](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders) · [Placement rule](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:88)

The practice house bots “share vision, never fight each other, and hunt you weakest first.” One engages early, more join as you thin the squad out, and all engage late. Outlasting the clock for some portion of a match improves your score only if house bots die before you or the survivor ranking resolves in your favor. A 1.15 mean is roughly 14.85th place. A gain of two placements per match adds two points to that mean. [Track rules](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders)

If fewer than 32 teams enter, all enter qualifying, unless there are 16 or fewer, in which case everyone goes straight to the series. Expert house bots fill vacancies. The organizers can change match counts before final play starts. A qualifying entry that cannot be fetched or rebuilt loses its seat to the next practice-board entry. ## Bot contract and limits

Use one UTF-8 JSON object per newline on stdin and stdout. Keep each raw line at or under 65,536 bytes, including any trailing spaces, and flush each reply. Send diagnostics to stderr. Runtime match stderr is unavailable to contestants per the protocol, although a failed check can show its tail. Wrapper comments saying stderr is shown after matches conflict with the rulebook. [Transport and runtime](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:75)

| Step | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Server opens | `{"type":"hello","protocol":"mechfight/1","slot":3,"seed":123,"map":{"dir":"/arena","files":["v2/world.json","v2/terrain.i16"]},"config":{…}}`. `slot` is 0–15. `seed` is a deterministic RNG seed fixed per slot. The actual match seed is private. `config` contains defaults for this match, including tick rates, all mechanics and upgrade definitions. |
| Bot starts | `{"type":"ready","name":"my-bot"}` within `hello_budget_ms` of 20 seconds including startup. `name` is optional in the schema and truncated to 32 characters in the engine. |
| Server updates | `{"type":"obs","tick":N,"t":…,"alive":…,"self":…,"mechs":[…],"projectiles":[…],"crates":[…],"cubes":[…],"pickups":[…],"sounds":[…],"beams":[…],"events":[…],"drops":[…],"zone":…,"offer":…}`. One decision every two 20 Hz simulation ticks. |
| Bot acts | `{"tick":N,"move":[0.7,-0.7],"aim":[0.95,-0.05],"fire":[true,false]}`. Send one action and echo the exact `tick`. `type:"action"` is optional in the schema. |
| Server ends | `{"type":"end","place":P,"kills":K}`, then stdin closes. No new observations after death. Exit on `end` and handle EOF. |

`obs.self` has id, feet position, velocity, actual torso aim, HP and max HP, energy and max energy, grounded and in-zone flags, cube count, upgrade IDs, current derived mech stats and exactly two weapon slots. A slot is `null` or has `kind`, `cooldown`, `stats`, and where applicable `ammo`, `reload`, or `charge`. `mechs` combines enemy mechs and NPCs. Each has `id`, `kind`, `pos`, `vel`, and `aim`. HP, max HP, cubes, held weapons, and laser charging are conditional. Use live `hello.config`, `self.stats`, and slot `stats` for balance and upgrades. Ignore additional protocol fields. [Message reference](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:109) · [JSON Schema](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/mechfight.schema.json)

Projectiles have id, kind, position and velocity. Crates and cubes carry id and position, with HP on crates. Pickups add weapon kind. Sounds carry kind, world bearing, range bucket and age. Beams carry shooter id, kind, path and age. Events since the last observation cover damage, hit, weapon effects, kills, cube pickup, upgrade, supply drop capture, weapon take, weapon drop and failed take. `zone` carries current and next centers and radii, shrink window and DPS. Global `drops` includes position, radius, time to land, capture duration, progress and holder. `offer` is null or has id, expiry, and choices. [Observation fields and event details](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:138)

| Action field | Behavior and default |
| --- | --- |
| `tick` | Required. Latest observation tick. Each action lasts two sim ticks. |
| `move: [x,y]` | Desired world-space horizontal direction with length capped at 1. Shorter length means lower speed. Default `[0,0]`. |
| `aim: [yaw,pitch]` | Desired torso heading in radians. The real aim slews. Pitch is clamped. Default is previous commanded aim. |
| `boost`, `jump` | Default `false`. Boost accelerates ground movement or runs the upward jet in air. Jump requires a grounded mech. |
| `fire: [bool,bool]`, `reload` | Two held trigger states by slot, default `[false,false]`. Reload requests early gun reload, default `false`. |
| `pick`, `take`, `drop` | Optional upgrade choice index 0–2, pickup id, and weapon slot 0 or 1 to empty. `take` with `drop` replaces a slot. |
| `debug` | Optional drawings. Defaults cap at 64 lines, 64 points, 16 texts, 80 characters per text or label, 8,192 serialized bytes per action and 8,000,000 per match. The server trims excess. The track gives teams no viewer for their drawings. |

Coordinate frame is x east, y north, z up, in meters. Yaw is counter-clockwise from east and pitch is up-positive. Default torso yaw and pitch slews are 4 and 3 rad/s. Default pitch limits are −0.7 and +1.3 rad. The mech is a cylinder with radius 2.5 m and height 9 m at spawn. Baseline eye height is 8 m, torso muzzle height 7 m, and mortar tube height 9.5 m, all measured above the feet. Shots start about 3 m along the actual aim, limited by nearby geometry. Recheck muzzle and size after cube growth. [Coordinates](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:96) · [Action reference](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:216)

The server sends the active bots their observations together, waits for replies up to 50 ms, applies actions, then advances 0.1 simulated seconds. The 50 ms budget is “measured by the server from sending `obs` to reading your line.” Wrong or stale ticks, invalid JSON and oversize lines are dropped. On a missed valid reply, the engine keeps the previous move, boost and aim for 1 simulated second, drops the other action fields, and then idles holding aim. An exited process idles for the rest of the match and still receives a placement for scoring. Consume only the newest queued observation. Use elapsed sim ticks for cooldown logic and bound wall-clock work with time left to serialize and flush. Seed randomness from `hello.seed` and use stable ordering. Near-limit response times can yield different scores on reruns. The rules publish no separate nondeterminism penalty. [Match timing](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:52) · [Track runtime limits](https://www.joinbuilders.org/buildathon/tracks/builders)

| Stage | Published limits |
| --- | --- |
| Build | `linux/amd64`, network available, 10 minutes, repository at most 20 MB at the selected commit. Pin base image and downloads for the final rebuild. |
| Runtime | Docker, one CPU, 512 MB RAM, no swap, at most 128 processes and threads, uid/gid `65534:65534` in the published run command, no capabilities, no network. Read-only root and map, writable `/tmp` up to 64 MB. Use system-wide dependencies. |

The checker feeds one `hello`, 300 recorded observations and an `end`, batching three observations every 25th step. The fixture covers ticks 0–736, or 36.8 match seconds, with at most one observed actor per decision, and no upgrade offers or supply drops. To add coverage, write `--stream` cases with correct protocol JSON lines in gzip. Actions cannot affect the recorded input or its ending place. The protocol explicitly says “`check/check.py` is not a simulation.” [Checker source](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/check/check.py:141) · [Recorded observations](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/check/stream.jsonl.gz) · [Round-trip check rules](mech-royale/starter/protocol.md:27)

More than 2% missed replies fails a pass (seven or more of 300). The checker can retry a timing-only failure with fresh processes up to three total passes. The shipped code accepts the first passing attempt and reports its last attempt if all fail. Schema problems, wrong ticks and exiting during play are conformance failures. Older queued ticks are discarded and counted. Two-second exit overruns and debug-cap overruns produce warnings in the shipped code. The checker also waits for exit after `end` without inspecting the exit status. Treat warnings or wrapper exceptions as failures for your own release gate. The wrappers can catch initialization errors and still send `ready`, and they can return a tick-only action or previous controls on policy errors. Check stderr and assert meaningful actions in targeted scenarios. Serialize finite numbers even if your language's JSON library allows something else. ## First improvements to test

1. **Stop getting blocked and caught by the zone.** The examples steer directly toward the next center and jump every 40 ticks. Give the bot a nearby safe waypoint, a wall-clearance check and a recovery move when observed displacement stalls. Treat a roof as a useful perch when it has a workable approach and exit. Save energy for unblocking and escaping. Use the remaining time and predicted path to decide when to rotate.
2. **Lead shots and keep the torso facing danger.** The examples aim at current position and hold both triggers whenever a mech appears. For the gun, solve the first positive intercept time against target velocity from the muzzle, aim within slew limits, and release fire when terrain blocks the ray or the weapon is out of range. Use visible projectiles to avoid clear impact paths when a lateral step avoids the hit. Preserve your front arc during retreat independently of leg movement.
3. **Make the second slot count.** Add a laser hold/release controller and lightning range/LOS checks. The starter only takes nearby pickups if slot 1 is empty, ignores vertical reach during selection, and never swaps. Assign value to a reachable secondary along the safe route. Ignore distant and vertically inaccessible loot unless pathing can reach it in time.
4. **Reset damaged fights.** Break LOS, stop dealing damage long enough to heal, reload, and re-engage from cover before the zone cuts off the route. Take low-risk crates for the first upgrade. Prioritize armor, speed, regen or weapon upgrades based on the fight pattern and current loadout. Check weapon timers and line of sight before committing to a supply drop.

These are hypotheses to test. The baseline's source confirms the missing behaviors, but there is no measured score for any improvement. [Python policy](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/python/example_bot.py) · [TypeScript policy](mech-royale/starter/mechfight-kit/ts/example.ts)

## Likely winning approach and risks

Use a deterministic policy with two timescales. Every 0.3–1.0 s of simulated time, or immediately after a damage spike or zone change, rank reachable objectives by travel time, zone margin, exposure, cover quality, energy needed and value of loot or repairs. Each 0.1 s decision, score a small set of movement commands by expected near-term position, collision risk, incoming projectiles and lines of fire. Allow the combat controller to aim and fire while the movement controller strafes or retreats. Keep the set of candidate moves and the geometry budget bounded.

Against the squad, exploit the early single-attacker window. Draw the first attacker into a short fight where cover limits exposure, secure a weapon and cubes, then restore HP before facing more bots. Re-route early as the squad joins. Getting a few actual eliminations is valuable because the practice squad won't normally create them for you through fighting itself. Roof access may help isolate ground pursuers, but the house bots' jet behavior, target selection and pathing are unknown. Test ground-only navigation first, then use roof routes only when they improve results.

Against other finalists, approach safely from the edge of the next circle with a short route inward. Let opponents commit to fights, pressure exposed low-HP targets, collect the resulting loot when safe, and leave once multiple opponents can shoot you. Aim to arrive at the last usable cover with high HP and a mobility reserve. Laser plus gun is a reasonable open-range default, and lightning gains value as the circle gets small or charging opponents get close. Mortar is worth adding when ballistic collision and self-splash tests work, especially if short walls dominate the final area. Treat loadout and position choices as conditional rather than forcing one strategy every seed.

The main risks are overfitting a fixed squad, trading too much HP for early loot, getting stranded on a roof, relying on off-screen tracking as shot LOS, and optimizing a local model with incorrect movement or projectile order. A tiny proxy model is enough to reject obvious failures. Use the time left to improve the bot on official measurements and stress scenarios with multiple competing opponents. No documented observation field identifies the scoring round, so one selected entry must adapt to what it observes.

## Build plan for three to five coding agents

Use Python and the starter's standard-library wrapper as the initial integration choice. It already passes the native check and gives a quick implementation path. Keep the shared units, policy state and interface signatures in one module, and make exactly one module assemble the outgoing action.

Track startup, response p50/p99/max, latency misses, invalid actions, wrapper errors and memory. |
| 2. Geometry and movement | Own `Arena` with `height(x,y)`, `shot_clear(a,b)` and `plan(start,goal,clearance) -> waypoints`. `navigate(obs, intent, threats) -> {move, boost, jump, eta, blocked}`. Use spatial indexing, ground waypoints and explicit vertical edges only where tested. | Test a building corner, bridge with clearance and low underside, differing cube sizes, a roof approach and landing, and rotation around a blocking wall before shrink. |
| 3. Combat and threat estimates | `assess(obs, arena) -> Threats` gives projectile risks and plausible lines of fire. `combat(obs, arena, intent, threats) -> {aim, fire, reload}`. Own lead, slew, cover, target selection and per-slot weapon state. | Moving-target intercept, actual aim crossing a beam line, held and released laser, obstruction, reload in cover and lightning target competition. |
| 4. Objectives and inventory | `choose_intent(obs, arena, threats) -> Intent`, with goal, desired range, engage flag and retreat urgency. `inventory(obs, intent) -> {pick, take, drop}`. Own zone-horizon, health and energy tradeoffs, upgrades and supply drop priorities. | Rotation versus late loot, contested supply drop, disengagement to heal, unavailable swap and changing upgrade value. |
| 5. Evaluation | Own synthetic fixture generation, replay checks, isolated timing runs, scenario seeds, result CSVs and paired comparisons. With three agents, combine runtime with evaluation and combine objectives with geometry and movement. With four, combine runtime and evaluation only.
